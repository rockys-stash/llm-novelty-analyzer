# Plan: LLM Novelty vs Recombination Analyzer

> Written before the experiments ran; changes made during the build are recorded in
> `docs/DECISIONS.md`.

## 1. Question

Are research ideas generated by language models meaningfully new, or mostly recombinations of
existing knowledge? Semantic similarity does not prove novelty, so the study does not stop at
measuring similarity. It asks whether computational indicators of novelty **correlate with human
judgments of novelty**.

Pipeline: **LLM-generated idea → concept extraction → literature retrieval → semantic comparison →
prior-art mapping → novelty indicators**, then indicators against judgments.

## 2. Material

- **Literature.** Six research topics; for each, up to 1,000 of the most relevant journal articles
  in Crossref published 2015–2025 that have an abstract and a deposited reference list. A DOI
  manifest fixes the snapshot.
- **Generated ideas.** Two local open-weights models (Qwen3-4B-Instruct, Phi-4-mini-instruct) each
  propose 8 research ideas per topic (96 ideas).
- **Controls with known provenance**, written in the same Title/Idea format by the same model so
  that they differ from the ideas in where their content comes from, not in style:
  - *paraphrase*: a corpus paper restated as an idea (its prior art is in the corpus);
  - *recombination*: two corpus papers combined into one idea;
  - *later*: a paper published in 2026, after the snapshot, restated as an idea (its content is
    not in the corpus).
  Six of each per topic (108 controls).

## 3. Indicators

Semantic similarity (highest, and mean of the 10 nearest), keyword overlap, concept overlap with
the nearest papers, citation overlap (bibliographic coupling of the nearest papers),
knowledge-graph measures over a concept co-occurrence graph (share of the idea's concept pairs
already combined in some paper, and their association), and the share of new phrases
(`docs/METRICS.md`).

## 4. Research questions and hypotheses

| | Question | Hypothesis | Experiment |
|---|---|---|---|
| RQ1 | How close are generated ideas to the existing literature, by each indicator? | Generated ideas sit between restated papers and later papers. | E1 |
| RQ2 | Do the indicators pass a test with known answers? | Every indicator ranks paraphrases below later papers (AUROC > 0.5); semantic similarity does so best. Recombinations are harder to separate from later papers. | E2 |
| RQ3 | Do the indicators correlate with judgments of novelty? | Correlations with human novelty scores are positive but modest. (Human study: pending until collected; a model-judge pilot exercises the analysis.) | E3 |
| RQ4 | Do the indicators measure the same thing? | No: semantic similarity and concept-pair novelty disagree; many ideas are dissimilar in wording but combine already-combined concepts. | E4 |
| RQ5 | Do the results depend on the embedding model or corpus size? | Rankings are stable (Spearman > 0.8) across embedding models; smaller corpora raise apparent novelty. | E5 |

## 5. Human evaluation

Annotators score sampled items for novelty, relevance and meaningfulness on 1–5 scales in the
console, blind to the item's kind and indicators (`docs/ANNOTATION_PROTOCOL.md`). If the full
study cannot be run here, the human study is marked pending and the correlation analysis is
exercised on a clearly labelled model-judge pilot.

## 6. Deliverables

Python package `novelty`; committed items and judgments; experiments E1–E5 with versioned results;
`docs/REPORT.md`; the **LLM Idea Novelty Explorer** console with an annotation page; tests; CI.

## 7. Risks

- The literature is one search in one index; relevant work outside it is invisible to every
  indicator.
- Small 4-bit models write the ideas; the conclusions are about these models.
- Model judges share training data and biases with the generators.
